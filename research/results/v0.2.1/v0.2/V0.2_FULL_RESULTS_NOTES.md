# Benchmark v0.2 — Full Experiment Re-Run (Ablation, Calibration, Safety, Functional, Taxonomy)

This completes the full re-run requested after `V0.2_RESULTS_NOTES.md` (which covered only the
OOD/ambiguity/statistical-power question). **The most important finding in this document is not
a win — it is a non-replication that must be reported prominently.**

## The headline claim does not replicate at the same significance threshold on v0.2

| Comparison | v0.1 (n=135) | v0.2 (n=159) |
|---|---|---|
| A0 (BM25) accuracy | 71.9% | 75.5% |
| A3 (Hybrid) accuracy | 77.1% | 79.3% |
| A0 vs A3 discordant pairs | 7 (7 favor A3, 0 favor A0) | 8 (7 favor A3, **1 favors A0**) |
| A0 vs A3 exact McNemar's p | **0.0156 (significant)** | **0.0703 (NOT significant)** |
| A2 (Dense) vs A3 exact McNemar's p | 0.146 (not significant) | **0.0127 (significant)** |

**On v0.1, "hybrid significantly beats BM25" was the strongest, cleanest claim in the whole
program. On v0.2, that specific claim no longer clears p<0.05** — verified at the per-query level
(not a computation bug): of 8 discordant non-OOD pairs, 7 still favor the hybrid system, but one
new case (`TA-B194`, "sudo reboot" vs "sudo shutdown -h now" — a genuinely close call) now favors
BM25, and the larger denominator (159 vs 135, driven by the 24 new hard ambiguous queries) is
enough to push the p-value above the conventional threshold.

**Simultaneously, the opposite comparison flips: hybrid vs. dense-alone, not significant on v0.1
(p=0.146), IS significant on v0.2 (p=0.0127).**

### Why this happened, honestly assessed

The 24 new v0.2 ambiguous queries deliberately include hard, adversarial, short technical-keyword
cases (`grep`, `sed`, `awk`, `tar`, `jq`, `ffmpeg`, `aws`, etc. — Section on "Ambiguous candidates"
in `v0.2_ADJUDICATION_REPORT.md`). These are exactly the kind of query where BM25's exact-token
matching is strong (a short, distinctive technical term matches its corresponding command family
tightly), which plausibly narrows BM25's disadvantage relative to dense/hybrid retrieval on this
specific, harder slice — while dense retrieval's relative weakness on these same short, jargon-
heavy queries (embeddings are typically weaker at discriminating near-synonym short technical
terms than at general semantic similarity) widens the hybrid-vs-dense gap. This is a plausible,
literature-consistent explanation (short/technical-term retrieval is a known BM25 strength), but
it is offered as an interpretation, not verified as the sole cause — a fuller investigation (e.g.
an ablation restricted to only the new hard keywords) is noted as future work, not undertaken
here without evidence it would resolve anything not already stated.

### What the paper must now say (this supersedes the Phase 17 decision gate's original framing)

- **Cannot claim**, without qualification, "hybrid significantly beats BM25" — that holds on v0.1
  (p=0.016) but not on the larger, harder v0.2 benchmark (p=0.070). The honest claim is: *the
  accuracy improvement is directionally consistent and numerically similar in magnitude across
  both benchmark versions (+5.2pp on v0.1, +3.8pp on v0.2), but statistical significance for the
  BM25-vs-hybrid comparison specifically is sensitive to which benchmark version is used, and does
  not robustly replicate at p<0.05 on the harder, more diverse v0.2 query set.*
- **Can now claim**, newly, on v0.2: hybrid significantly beats dense-alone (p=0.013) — the
  reverse of what could be claimed on v0.1.
- **The OOD result strengthens and remains robust** (Section in `V0.2_RESULTS_NOTES.md`):
  p=0.000015, unambiguous.
- **This is exactly the outcome a rigorous benchmark-expansion exercise is supposed to produce**:
  neither a clean double-confirmation nor a clean refutation, but a more precise, harder-won
  understanding of which claims are robust (OOD detection, dense-vs-hybrid on the harder set) and
  which are benchmark-scale/composition-sensitive (the original headline BM25-vs-hybrid claim).

## Other re-run results (ablation, calibration, safety)

### Ablation A0–A6 (`research/results/v0.2/ablation-results.json`)

| Condition | v0.1 | v0.2 |
|---|---:|---:|
| A0 (BM25) | 71.9% | 75.4% |
| A1 (BM25, no bonus) | 71.9% (identical to A0) | 75.4% (identical to A0 — bonus still has zero effect) |
| A2 (Dense) | 72.7% | 72.4% |
| A3 (Hybrid) | 77.1% | 79.3% |
| A4 (+margin rejection) | 88.5% @ 70.8% coverage | 76.1% @ 64.1% coverage |
| A5 (+OOD detection) | 90.1% @ 69.5% coverage, 10/15 OOD caught | 82.2% @ 58.9% coverage, 38/50 OOD caught |
| A6 (+calibration) | same as A5 + ECE 0.054 | same as A5 + ECE 0.074 |

A4/A5's selective accuracy is *lower* on v0.2 than v0.1 (82.2% vs 90.1%) at *lower* coverage
(58.9% vs 69.5%) — reported plainly, not spun as an improvement. This is consistent with v0.2's
harder, more diverse query mix making the accept/reject decision genuinely harder, not with any
implementation regression (the substring-bonus null result replicates exactly, a good sanity
check that the pipeline itself is behaving consistently).

### Calibration (`research/results/v0.2/calibration-results.json`)

| Variant | v0.1 ECE reduction | v0.2 ECE reduction |
|---|---:|---:|
| baseline_confidence | 56.7% | **84.1%** |
| margin_confidence | 67.2% | **83.2%** |
| semantic_confidence | 47.0% | 59.6% |
| hybrid_reliability | **80.4%** | 77.2% |

All four variants still improve substantially on v0.2; three improve by a larger margin than on
v0.1, one (hybrid_reliability) by a slightly smaller margin. No isotonic tie-handling errors were
triggered (the permanent assertion added after Phase 8's bug remained silent), confirming that
fix generalizes correctly to the larger dataset.

### Safety (`research/results/v0.2/safety-eval-results.json`)

| | v0.1 gold | v0.2 gold | v0.1 retrieved | v0.2 retrieved |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Exact 4-tier accuracy | 89.6% | 83.9% | 90.0% | 86.6% |
| Risky-binary precision | 0.95 | 0.95 | 0.826 | 0.769 |
| Risky-binary recall | 0.95 | 0.864 | 0.95 | 0.909 |

Accuracy and precision/recall shift somewhat on v0.2 because the classifier (deliberately not
re-tuned against new benchmark examples, per Phase 11's anti-test-set-tuning discipline) was
authored before the new ambiguous queries' gold commands existed, so some of them (e.g.
`Stop-Process -Name ... -Force` appearing as a gold command) fall into the same documented,
pre-existing classifier gaps from Phase 11 (`SAFETY_EVAL_NOTES.md`), not new ones.

### Functional evaluation

Unchanged and reconfirmed rather than re-executed: all 15 functional-eval queries are byte-
identical between v0.1 and v0.2, and their hybrid-system top-1 retrieved commands are also
identical (verified directly against both candidate caches) despite different fold structure —
so re-running the actual sandboxed command execution would reproduce the exact same 100%/93.3%
result with no new information. Not re-run, to avoid redundant real command execution.
